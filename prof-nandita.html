Honouring a Distinguished Solar Physicist

Prof. Nandita Srivastava

Senior Professor • Udaipur Solar Observatory • Physical Research Laboratory

Prof. Nandita Srivastava has devoted more than three decades to advancing our understanding of the Sun, particularly solar eruptions, coronal mass ejections (CMEs), heliophysics, and space weather. Biography

Prof. Nandita Srivastava is a Senior Professor at the Udaipur Solar Observatory (USO), Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad, India. She is internationally recognised for her pioneering contributions to solar physics, particularly in understanding coronal mass ejections (CMEs), solar eruptions, solar filaments, and the Sun–Earth connection.

Her fascination with astronomy began while growing up in a family of scholars in Madhya Pradesh. Inspired by books written by Prof. Jayant V. Narlikar and encouraged by her teachers, she discovered her passion for solar physics after attending a national solar workshop at Kodaikanal Observatory—an experience that shaped the course of her scientific career.

Prof. Srivastava joined the Udaipur Solar Observatory as a Ph.D. student, spending years observing the Sun from the observatory's unique island telescope on Fateh Sagar Lake. Following postdoctoral research abroad, she returned to USO, where she has played a leading role in advancing solar observations in India, including research associated with the Multi-Application Solar Telescope (MAST).

Her research focuses on the initiation and propagation of solar eruptions, coronal mass ejections, solar filaments, and their influence on Earth's space environment. Through observations and theoretical interpretation, she has contributed significantly to our understanding of space weather and the scientific foundations of India's heliophysics programme, including the Aditya-L1 mission.

Beyond research, Prof. Srivastava has mentored numerous students and early-career researchers, organised scientific meetings, strengthened international collaborations, and helped build a vibrant solar physics community in India. Her scientific leadership continues to inspire the next generation of researchers.

This symposium celebrates not only her distinguished scientific achievements but also her lasting impact as a mentor, collaborator, and leader in Indian solar and space weather research.

Career Journey

A distinguished academic and research career dedicated to advancing solar physics, heliophysics, and space weather research in India and worldwide.

Education

Completed her undergraduate studies with a B.Sc. in Physics from Guru Ghasidas University, Bilaspur (1983–1985), followed by a M.Sc. in Physics from Pt. Ravishankar Shukla University, Raipur (1985–1987).

Doctoral & Early Research

Prof. Srivastava completed her Ph.D. in Physics at the Udaipur Solar Observatory (USO), Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Udaipur during 1988–1993. She subsequently continued at USO–PRL as a Postdoctoral Fellow (September 1993 – July 1995), where she further developed her research in solar physics.

International Research Experience

Prof. Srivastava's research career has been enriched by extensive international research experience through appointments at leading institutions in Europe and South America. She served as a Visiting Scientist at the National Institute for Space Research (INPE), Brazil (August 1995 – June 1997), followed by a Postdoctoral Fellowship at the Max Planck Institute for Aeronomy, Germany (August 1997 – January 2000). She later joined the European Space Research and Technology Centre (ESTEC), European Space Agency (ESA), Noordwijk, The Netherlands as a Visiting Fellow (February 2000 – June 2000).

Physical Research Laboratory

Following her international research appointments, Prof. Srivastava returned to the Udaipur Solar Observatory (USO), Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), where she has spent more than two decades advancing solar physics research in India. Since joining the faculty as a Scientist SD in July 2000, she has steadily progressed through the academic ranks, serving as Reader (2004–2009), Associate Professor (2009–2014), Professor (2014–2019), and Senior Professor (2020–Present). In addition to her research and academic responsibilities, she served as the Deputy Head (Administration) of the Udaipur Solar Observatory from April 2015 to December 2022, contributing significantly to the institution's scientific and administrative development.

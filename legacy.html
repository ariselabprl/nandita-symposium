Scientific Legacy

Advancing Solar Physics and Space Weather

Over three decades of pioneering research on solar eruptions, coronal mass ejections, heliophysics, and the Sun–Earth connection, shaping modern space weather research in India and beyond.

Major Research Themes

Prof. Nandita Srivastava's research has significantly advanced our understanding of the physical processes governing solar eruptions and their impact throughout the heliosphere.

Coronal Mass Ejections

Understanding the initiation, evolution, propagation and geoeffectiveness of coronal mass ejections (CMEs), one of the most important drivers of severe space weather.

Solar Eruptions

Investigating solar flares, eruptive prominences, magnetic instabilities and the physical mechanisms responsible for energetic solar events.

Solar Filaments

Studying the formation, evolution and eruption of solar filaments and their relationship with coronal mass ejections.

Sun–Earth Connection

Exploring how solar activity propagates through the heliosphere and influences Earth's magnetosphere and near-Earth space environment.

Space Weather

Improving our understanding of space weather processes, forecasting, and the impacts of solar activity on technological systems.

Aditya-L1 Science

Supporting scientific investigations associated with India's first dedicated solar mission and future heliophysics research.

Scientific Journey

Over three decades of research advancing our understanding of solar eruptions, coronal mass ejections, and space weather.

1990s

Foundations in Solar Physics

Early research focused on solar activity, eruptive phenomena, and the observational study of the solar atmosphere, laying the foundation for future investigations of coronal mass ejections.

2000–2010

Coronal Mass Ejections

Major contributions to understanding CME initiation, propagation, and their relationship with solar filaments, solar flares, and interplanetary disturbances.

2010–Present

Sun–Earth Connection & Heliosphere

Research expanded toward the geoeffectiveness of CMEs, space weather forecasting, and understanding how solar eruptions affect Earth's near-space environment.

Major Scientific Contributions

Representative areas where Prof. Srivastava's research has made significant contributions to solar and heliospheric physics.

Understanding CME Initiation

Research aimed at understanding how coronal mass ejections are triggered, evolve in the solar corona, and become major drivers of heliospheric disturbances.

CME Propagation

Studies of CME propagation through interplanetary space and the factors controlling their arrival and impact at Earth.

Solar Filament Eruptions

Investigations of eruptive filaments and prominences, providing insight into the physical connection between filament eruptions and coronal mass ejections.

Operational Space Weather

Contributions linking basic solar physics with practical space weather forecasting and applications relevant to modern technological systems.

Collaborations

International Collaborations

Extensive collaborations with leading international space science communities through missions, joint projects and research programmes.

  • Guest Investigator, ESA–PROBA2 mission for solar observations using SWAP and LYRA instruments.
  • Principal Investigator, Indo–US IUSSTF Virtual Network Center project on CME propagation and prediction of magnetic field Bz in interplanetary space (2021–2024).
  • Principal Investigator, India–Uzbekistan joint project on "Space Weather Consequences of Coronal Mass Ejections" (2021–2024).
  • Member, International Space Science Institute (ISSI) team on "Understanding Stealth CMEs and Problem Geomagnetic Storms" (2017–2019).
  • Member, International Study for Earth-Affecting Solar Transients (ISEST) programme with scientists from USA, Europe, Japan, China and other countries.
  • External collaborator in ESA FP7 COMESEP project on forecasting space weather impacts of CMEs and Solar Energetic Particles (2011–2014).
  • Collaborator in NSF-funded international projects on CME initiation and solar eruptive processes.

National Collaborations

Contributions to major Indian solar and space science missions, ISRO programmes and national research initiatives.

  • Co-PI, Venus Radiation Environment Monitor (VeRad) experiment of PRL Venus Orbiter Mission (VOM), ISRO.
  • Member, Science Team of MAG, ASPEX and SUIT instruments on India's first solar mission Aditya-L1.
  • Co-PI, ISRO–RESPOND project on "Solar Flares: Physics and Forecasting for Better Understanding of Space Weather" (2023–2026).
  • Co-PI, ISRO–RESPOND project on automatic detection of solar features including sunspots and filaments (2020–2023).
  • Principal Investigator, ISRO-funded CAWSES project on "Pre-eruptive Phase of Solar Filaments" (2005–2008).
  • Contributed to the proposal development of the Multi Application Solar Telescope (MAST) project at PRL.
  • Contributor to national initiatives in computational space sciences and solar-terrestrial physics.

A Lasting Scientific Legacy

Prof. Nandita Srivastava's career has significantly advanced our understanding of solar eruptions, coronal mass ejections, space weather, and the Sun–Earth connection.

Her scientific achievements extend beyond an exceptional publication record. Through mentorship, international collaborations, scientific leadership, and community building, she has helped shape modern solar physics research in India.

The impact of her work continues through the students she has mentored, the collaborations she has fostered, and the scientific questions that her research has inspired.
